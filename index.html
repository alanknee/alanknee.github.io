About me

I am an astrophysicist specializing in gravitational waves. My research focuses on analyzing data from gravitational-wave observatories to learn about the most extreme objects in the Universe, such as black holes and neutron stars.

I obtained a Ph.D. in astronomy from the University of British Columbia in 2025, supervised by Dr. Jess McIver. I am currently a postdoctoral research fellow at the University of Michigan. I have been an active member of the LIGO Scientific Collaboration since 2019.

Lately my work has focused heavily on the search for continuous gravitational waves emitted by rapidly rotating neutron stars that sustain long-lived deformations. I deploy large-scale data analysis pipelines to search for these elusive signals and develop techniques to make our searches more sensitive. I am also interested in the observational capabilities of the future space-based LISA detector, which will provide new opportunities to study the formation and evolution of compact binaries.

Please contact me at aknee@umich.edu if you'd like to get in touch.

Research Interests

  • Searches for continuous gravitational waves from rapidly rotating neutron stars
  • Gravitational-wave parameter estimation for compact binary systems
  • LIGO detector characterization and data quality validation
  • High-throughput computing
