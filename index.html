About me

I am a professional astrophysicist specializing in gravitational waves. My research focuses on analyzing data from gravitational-wave observatories in order to learn more about extreme objects, such as black holes and neutron stars.

I obtained a Ph.D. in astronomy from the University of British Columbia in 2025, supervised by Dr. Jess McIver. I am currently a postdoctoral research fellow at the University of Michigan. I have been an active member of the LIGO Scientific Collaboration since 2019.

Lately my work has focused heavily on the search for continuous gravitational waves (CWs) emitted by rapidly rotating neutron stars that sustain long-lived deformations. I build and deploy large-scale data analysis pipelines in an effort to search for these elusive signals. My work includes wide-parameter-space all-sky searches, searches that account for irregular phase evolution, pulsar glitches, and detector characterization. I have also been interested in the observational capabilities of the future space-based LISA detector, which will provide new opportunities to study the formation and evolution of compact binaries.

Please contact me at aknee@umich.edu if you'd like to chat.

Research interests

  • Astrophysics: Gravitational waves from spinning neutron stars, merging compact binaries, hierarchical triple systems
  • Data analysis: Gravitational-wave data analysis/signal processing, matched filter searches, hidden Markov models, Bayesian parameter estimation
  • Detector physics: LIGO detector characterization, data quality assessment
